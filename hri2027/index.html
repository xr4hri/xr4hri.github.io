Call for papers

We invite researchers, students, and practitioners working at the intersection of extended reality and robotics to submit to the 9th Workshop on Extended Reality for Human-Robot Interaction (XR4HRI), formerly VAM-HRI, held at ACM/IEEE HRI 2027 in Santa Clara.

Humans and robots working together need a shared understanding of goals, intentions, and context. XR offers a shared spatial medium for this: it can show a robot's planned motion before it happens, reveal its internal state, support remote teleoperation, and let researchers prototype robot behaviour before any hardware exists. In line with the HRI 2027 theme Innovative HRI, this edition focuses on moving XR interfaces from passive visualizations towards adaptive, bi-directional, and AI-enhanced interaction between people and robots.

We welcome early-stage work, position papers, and contributions from all disciplines and career stages, including first-time authors.

Topics

Topics include, but are not limited to:

Technologies and concepts

  • Registration and tracking
  • Visualizing data, intent, and feedback
  • Interaction concepts
  • Haptics and pseudo-haptics
  • AI- and LLM-enhanced XR interfaces
  • Safety, usability, and user experience
  • Robotic virtual twins
  • Transitional and cross-reality interfaces

Robots and application domains

  • Stationary, mobile, flying, humanoid, and zoomorphic robots
  • Teleoperated and shared-control settings
  • Industrial, construction, and medical applications
  • Automotive and consumer applications
  • Assistive and social robotics

How to submit

  • Papers are 2–6 pages including references, in the ACM sigconf format.
  • We welcome position papers, early-stage or late-breaking results, system descriptions, and demos.
  • Submit via OpenReview. Each submission receives at least two reviews from the program committee.
  • Accepted papers are non-archival and are published on OpenReview and this website, so you can still submit the work to a conference or journal later.
  • Every accepted paper is presented as a three-minute lightning talk and as a poster.
  • If you would like to show a live demo, please say so in your submission and describe your space, power, and hardware needs. Demos should be portable (e.g., headsets, tablets, small tabletop robots).
  • At least one author of each accepted paper attends the workshop, in person or remotely.

Program

  1. Welcome and icebreaker 10 min
  2. Invited keynote and discussion 30 min
  3. Lightning talks for all accepted papers 90 min
  4. Poster and demo session with coffee 40 min
  5. Breakout groups on the grand challenges of XR for HRI 35 min
  6. Group reports and panel discussion 25 min
  7. Wrap-up and next steps 10 min

Remote participants can join every session, including a dedicated online breakout group.
